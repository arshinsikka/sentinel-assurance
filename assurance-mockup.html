About this demo

What you are looking at, where the numbers come from, and how to test it with your own changes.

What this is

A working demo of Sentinel for a fictional hospital. Every figure is calculated in your browser from a sample file of made-up cases: for each case, what the AI said and what the hospital's clinician later concluded. Nothing is sent anywhere.

In a real hospital, Sentinel would run inside the hospital's own systems or its UAE cloud, reading the same two things from records the hospital already holds, so patient records never leave it.

How the figures are worked out

  • Catch rate (sensitivity): of the cases the clinician confirmed, the share the AI flagged.
  • Correctly left alone (specificity): of the cases the clinician ruled out, the share the AI did not flag.
  • Four-week windows: each weekly figure covers the last four weeks, so one unusual week does not raise a false alarm.
  • Patterns: a group is called a likely pattern only when it has at least 30 confirmed cases, is at least 5 points below the rest, and the difference is too large to be chance (a two-proportion test at the 99% level).
  • Risk ratings: the Department of Health's Responsible AI Risk Management Protocol (October 2025), using its rating table.

Test it yourself

Change the sample in any spreadsheet, then drop it here or click to choose it. Every page recalculates.

Columns in the file

system_id, date (YYYY-MM-DD), ai_output and clinician_finding (1 or 0; for the record assistant, the reviewer's verdict: ok, minor_error or major_error), age_group, sex, nationality_group. Optional: scanner and finding_type, used for "Where it fails".

Real-world reference points

Published US approval summaries for bleed-detection AI report catching about 93% of bleeds (Aidoc BriefCase, 2018: 93.6%; Viz ICH, 2020: 93%). When Michigan Medicine checked a widely used sepsis tool on its own patients, the vendor had reported 76 to 83% accuracy; the hospital found 63% (JAMA Internal Medicine, 2021).
